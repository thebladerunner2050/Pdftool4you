1. The Security Landscape of Password-Protected PDFs

Financial institutions, credit card providers, HR departments, and government bodies commonly password-protect PDF attachments before emailing them. Salary slips, bank account statements, tax returns (Form 16, ITR), and insurance policies are usually locked with a password generated from your Date of Birth (DOB) or PAN number.

While this protects files during email transit, entering your password every time you view or organize a statement quickly becomes frustrating. Furthermore, locked PDFs cannot be merged, split, or edited in document workflows until the password prompt is permanently removed.

2. Understanding User Passwords vs. Owner Permissions Locks

PDF specifications (ISO 32000) define two distinct security flags within the document catalog:

User / Open Password

Restricts Document Access

Encrypts the entire PDF object stream. Without entering the correct password string, the PDF reader cannot derive the decryption key or render a single page.

Owner / Permissions Lock

Restricts Actions (Print/Copy)

Allows viewing the document but sets permission bits to block printing, copying text, extracting pages, or filling interactive form fields.
