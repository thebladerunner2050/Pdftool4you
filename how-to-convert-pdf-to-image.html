Why Convert PDF Pages to Images?

While PDF is the standard for document exchange, many digital workflows require standard bitmap image formats like JPG, PNG, or WebP. Common scenarios include:

  • Social Media & Messaging Sharing: PDF documents cannot be previewed natively in apps like WhatsApp, Telegram, or Instagram stories.
  • Presentation & Web Embedding: Slide decks (PowerPoint, Keynote) and websites require images rather than embedded multi-page document streams.
  • Government & Portal Submissions: Certain admissions, tax, or visa portals strictly accept JPEG or PNG attachments under specific KB or DPI limits.
  • Design & Editing: Graphic designers need individual page assets in Photoshop, Figma, or Canva to crop or overlay branding.

Choosing the Right Image Format: JPG vs PNG vs WebP

Format Best For Compression Type Relative File Size
JPG / JPEG Scanned documents, photos, general sharing Lossy (Adjustable 10%-100%) Smallest (Lightweight)
PNG Text-heavy documents, charts, sharp line art Lossless (Zero quality drop) Medium-Large
WebP Website publishing, high-performance web apps Modern Lossy / Lossless 30% Smaller than JPG

Understanding 150 DPI vs 300 DPI Resolution

DPI (Dots Per Inch) determines how many pixels are generated for every inch of the PDF page:

150 DPI (Standard Digital)

Renders standard A4 pages at ~1240 x 1754 pixels. Perfect for quick email attachments, screen viewing, and messaging apps.

300 DPI (High-Definition Print)

Renders standard A4 pages at ~2480 x 3508 pixels. Essential for official printouts, fine typography, and archival quality.
